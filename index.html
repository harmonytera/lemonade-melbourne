Why two appointments

I'd rather build colour up than try to take it away.

Nobody can tell you exactly how pigment will heal in your skin. Not me, not anyone.

Your skin condition, your undertone, your immune system and how you heal all have a say, and none of them show up until afterwards. So I don't try to get it perfect in one sitting. I go softer and lighter than where we're heading, let your skin heal completely and show me what it did, then refine with evidence instead of a guess.

If you're frightened of ending up too dark, you shouldn't be asked to gamble on the first pass. This is how I make sure you aren't.

  1. Start soft

    Your first treatment lands deliberately a touch lighter than the finished result.

  2. Let your skin answer

    Six to eight weeks of healing shows me what your skin held onto.

  3. Refine with the evidence

    I adjust colour, density and shape based on what actually healed.

Why this is different work

A mature brow isn't a young brow in a softer colour.

The women who need the most care are rarely who the industry is built around. Most brow training, most of what you see online, most of the shapes people ask me for — designed for skin and faces thirty years younger than yours.

Your brow bone sits differently now. Your lid has softened. One brow has probably always been higher, you just didn't used to notice. A shape that looks lovely on your daughter looks drawn-on and slightly startled on you.

On grey hair

Grey isn't a colour. It's about forty different ones.

A third to a half of the women I work with are grey or going grey, and almost all of them arrive braced for me to make their brows grey to match. I won't. Grey hair is cool in some women and warm in others, and brows literally made grey look ashy and lifeless against most skin.

I look at your skin's undertone, what colour your brows were before they faded, what's left of them, and how much definition you want to live with. The answer is usually warmer than you expect, and far less dramatic than you're afraid of.

No blocky brows  ·  No template shapes  ·  No pressure to go darker  ·  Nothing that will date

The three techniques

The right one depends on your skin, not your preference.

People often ring having already decided, usually from Instagram. I'd rather look at your skin first. Oily skin won't hold a crisp hair stroke for long, thin skin needs a gentler hand, and how much natural hair you have changes everything. The price is the same whichever we land on.

Feathering

Nano hair strokes

A single fine needle, strokes drawn one at a time following the direction your own hair grows. Up close it reads as hair, not makeup.

Suits you if you still have some natural brow and your skin isn't especially oily.

Powder fill

Ombre powder

A fine mist of pigment instead of strokes — lighter at the front, deeper through the tail. Like you've filled them in gently and blended.

Suits you if your skin is oily, mature, thin or previously tattooed, or there's very little hair left.

Hair strokes + ombre

Combination brows

Strokes where the brow needs to look like hair, powder where it needs quiet density. Soft front, enough tail that the brow doesn't vanish.

Suits you if you want the most natural mature result — this is what I do most often.

No idea which you want? Completely normal, and not something you need to solve before you call. Choosing it is my job.

Questions

The questions women actually ask me

I'd rather answer the awkward ones now than have you lie awake on them later.

Is brow tattooing painful?

Most women tell me it was easier than they'd talked themselves into. Numbing cream before we start, topped up as we go, and breaks whenever you want one. Most describe it as scratchy and slightly odd rather than painful. Some women doze off.

What's the healing process like?

Darker and sharper for the first week — this is the part nobody warns you about and it's where people panic. Around day four to seven they flake and lighten, often dramatically, and by week two you'll be convinced they've gone. They haven't. Colour keeps surfacing for four to six weeks. What you see at six weeks is what you've got, which is exactly why I refine afterwards rather than before. Full healing timeline here.

What if I have very little or no natural brow hair?

You don't need existing hair for this to work. I use your bone structure, facial movement and careful mapping to build a shape that belongs on your face rather than sits on it. Some of my best work has been on women with nothing left at all.

How long will my brows last?

Typically 18 months to three years before you'd want them refreshed, depending on your skin, sun exposure and how your body breaks down pigment. They fade softly rather than going patchy or turning a strange colour — a function of the pigments and the technique.

Will my brows still suit me as I age?

The right question to ask, and very few people ask it. I design conservatively for exactly this reason. A shape that's too strong or too high looks increasingly out of place as a face continues to change, so I keep it soft enough to age alongside you. It's also why I don't do fashionable shapes.

How do I know if I'm a good candidate?

That's what the free call is for. Broadly: reasonable health, not on medication that affects healing or bleeding, and wanting a natural result rather than a dramatic one. Certain skin conditions, treatments and medications change the answer. I'll tell you honestly either way.

What if I don't know what style suits me?

Good — that's my job, not yours. Most women arrive with no idea, or an idea from Instagram that won't suit them. We work it out together, and you see the shape drawn on and approve it before anything permanent happens.

What if I was tattooed elsewhere and I'm not happy?

Send a clear photo with no makeup. Sometimes I can work over it, sometimes it needs fading or removal first, and occasionally the honest answer is to leave it alone. I'll tell you which, and I won't tattoo over something I don't think I can improve.

I have alopecia, or lost my brows through chemo, radiation or menopause — can I still have this done?

Often yes, and it's a significant part of my work. Timing matters, skin health matters, and if you're in or recently finished treatment I may need clearance from your doctor first. Please call regardless — even if the answer is “not yet”, you'll know what you're waiting for.

I work in healthcare and have a patient who'd benefit — how do I refer them?

Email me at excuseme@lemonadebrows.au. Happy to talk to you directly about whether it's appropriate, and I can speak with them without any obligation on their part. I volunteer with Look Good Feel Better and I'm comfortable working alongside a treating team.

Why are the forms due so far ahead?

Because anything that needs solving has to be solved before I leave Sydney. The health questionnaire tells me whether you're suitable and whether I need clearance from your doctor. The numbing cream form tells me whether you're safe with the agent I work with — and if you're not, that lead time is what lets me have something suitable compounded for you instead.

Uncommon, but it happens. If your form is back by 21 October I have time to get an alternative compounded. If that's not possible I'll tell you honestly, and you can decide whether you'd rather go ahead without numbing or not go ahead at all. I won't spring that on you in the chair.

If your forms came back by 21 October, you get your full deposit back. I'd much rather refund you than tattoo someone I shouldn't.

Do I need a patch test?

Not usually. Your health questionnaire and numbing clearance do that work — if nothing concerning comes up in either, we don't need one. If something does, I'll tell you what we need to do about it.

Yes. Upper Skin Clinic is registered for skin penetration procedures and has been running injectables there for fourteen years. I work to the same standards I do in Sydney, and I sit on the ABIC Regulation Committee, so this isn't something I'm casual about.
